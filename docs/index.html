A multi-label path planner can minimize the maximum continuous risk exposure along a route. Here exposure is measured in seconds spent in a designated risk zone. The objective minimizes the duration of the longest uninterrupted visit across feasible paths and timing profiles.

Exposure labels for a shared search state

At a shared search state, keep a label with two durations. The current duration C measures the ongoing visit. The maximum M records the longest completed or ongoing visit so far. On an inside segment of duration Δt, update C to C + Δt and M to max(M, C) using the updated C. On an outside segment, reset C to zero and carry M forward. Split a segment at the risk-zone boundary before applying these updates.

Suppose two partial paths reach the same state in a risk zone. Label A has C = 3 s and M = 3 s. Label B has C = 1 s and M = 4 s. After the same 2 s continuation inside the zone, A has M = 5 s and B has M = 4 s. This continuation reverses the ordering of the labels by maximum exposure. Retaining both partial paths preserves the alternatives for the search.

Salzman, Hou, and Srinivasa give a related example of history-dependent search in risk zones, using an accumulated cost that rises with the duration of ongoing exposure. ICAPS 2017 paper.

Path geometry and free space

A path p(s) specifies a curve in space using an ordered parameter s. Collision checking compares the curve and the vehicle footprint against the environment model. A safe corridor bounds the region in which a trajectory can be planned. Its construction and the collision model determine how much clearance the constraints provide.

A path through a narrow opening can be geometrically feasible even when some ways of traversing it demand more acceleration than the vehicle can produce. The time assigned to the maneuver is therefore part of evaluating the executable flight.

Time parameterization

A time law s(t) turns a geometric path into a trajectory p(s(t)). Velocity is the path tangent multiplied by the rate of progress. Acceleration has contributions from changing progress rate and from bending along the path. A fixed curve can support different speeds at different positions under the same acceleration limits.

Pham and Pham use reachability analysis to compute controllable and reachable sets along a discretized path. Their work illustrates time optimal path parameterization with a prescribed geometric path.

Spatial and temporal trajectory optimization

A trajectory optimizer can vary spatial parameters and segment durations together. Changing the shape can improve clearance or reduce curvature. Changing durations alters the time derivatives used to check velocity, acceleration and other dynamic constraints. The optimizer needs a model of the free space and the relevant vehicle constraints.

Wang and colleagues present a multicopter framework that uses a compact trajectory representation with spatial and temporal variation. Their formulation supports geometric conditions and several types of dynamic constraints.

Continuous risk exposure in robot path planning

Total risk exposure is the time spent in a defined risk region across all visits. The longest continuous exposure is the duration of the longest single visit. Four consecutive seconds and two separate visits of two seconds each both give four seconds of total exposure. Their longest uninterrupted exposures are four and two seconds, respectively. Changing the time law can change both measures along the same geometric path.

In ICAPS 2017, Salzman, Hou and Srinivasa studied a path cost with penalties that grow more than linearly with uninterrupted time inside a risk region. In a timed quadrotor comparison, define the risk region and its entry and exit rules, then report both durations alongside travel time and clearance.
